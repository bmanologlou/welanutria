Every January, millions of people start a crash diet. By February, most have stopped. The problem is not willpower, it is the approach: restriction and willpower run out, while **habits** stick. This guide is a calmer, more effective reset, built on adding good things rather than banning everything, and on routines you will still have in summer.

<div class="key-takeaway"><strong>The short answer</strong>Forget the crash diet. Add protein and fibre to every meal, eat at regular times, cook more at home, and move daily. These habits keep you full on fewer calories and actually last. Use a calorie target if you like numbers, but consistency matters more than any single rule.</div>

## Why "add", not "restrict"

The diets that fail share a pattern: they are built on taking things away. Cut carbs, cut fat, cut calories hard, and you are left hungry and running on willpower, which is exactly what gives out. The reset that works flips this. Instead of asking what to remove, it asks **what to add**, and the two most powerful additions are **protein and fibre**.

Both do the same crucial job: they keep you full on fewer calories. Protein also protects muscle, which keeps your metabolism up, and fibre feeds your gut and steadies energy. Build meals around them and appetite control largely takes care of itself, no white-knuckle restriction required. This is also why the approach suits the modern wave of [GLP-1 friendly eating](/blog/glp-1-friendly-foods/): it is the same protein-and-fibre-first logic.

## The four habits worth building

You do not need a dozen rules. Four habits carry most of the result.

**1. Protein at every meal.** Aim for around 1.2 to 1.6 g per kg of body weight if you are losing weight. Lead each meal with a protein source, from [high-protein foods](/food-lists/high-protein-foods/) like eggs, Greek yogurt, skyr, cottage cheese, chicken, fish, beans and tofu. Our [protein calculator](/calculators/protein/) sets your target.

**2. Fibre, built up gradually.** Work towards about 30 g a day from vegetables, fruit, wholegrains and legumes. The [high-protein high-fibre foods](/food-lists/high-protein-high-fiber-foods/) list is the sweet spot, hitting both targets at once. Increase slowly and drink more water as you do.

**3. Regular meals and home cooking.** Eating at consistent times and cooking more at home are two of the strongest predictors of keeping weight off. They take the guesswork, and a lot of hidden calories, out of your day.

**4. Daily movement.** Not punishing workouts, just consistent activity. A daily walk plus some resistance exercise twice a week protects muscle and supports the whole effort.

<div class="highlight"><p><strong>Why it matters:</strong> research on people who keep weight off points again and again to habits and routine, not short bursts of extreme effort. The boring, repeatable things are the ones that work.</p></div>

## Set your numbers (if you want them)

Some people thrive with a target; others do better focusing on food quality. If you like numbers, start with your [TDEE](/calculators/tdee/) to see your maintenance calories, apply a modest deficit with the [calorie deficit calculator](/calculators/calorie-deficit/), and split your intake with the [macros calculator](/calculators/macros/) to make sure protein stays high. Aim for a sustainable rate of around 0.5 kg a week, not a crash.

## The bottom line

A real January reset is not a punishment that ends in February. It is a handful of **sustainable habits**: protein and fibre at every meal, regular routines, home cooking and daily movement. Add rather than restrict, build the habits while motivation is high, and you will still have them when the new-year buzz has faded. Start with your [protein target](/calculators/protein/), pick a [meal plan](/meal-plans/) to follow, and build from there.
