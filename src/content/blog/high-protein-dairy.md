If you have walked down a supermarket dairy aisle lately, you will have noticed it: everything now shouts about **protein**. Cottage cheese, skyr, quark, Greek yogurt and kefir are all riding the same wave, and it can be hard to tell which one actually delivers the most. This guide settles it with numbers, then helps you choose the right one for your goal.

## The high-protein dairy ranking, per 100 g

Here is how the popular options compare on the two numbers that matter most, protein and calories, for the plain, low-fat versions:

- **Cottage cheese** (low-fat): around **12 g protein**, 72 kcal, 83 mg calcium
- **Quark** (low-fat): around **12 g protein**, 72 kcal, 90 mg calcium
- **Skyr**: around **11 g protein**, 63 kcal, 150 mg calcium
- **Greek yogurt** (0% fat): around **10 g protein**, 59 kcal, 110 mg calcium
- **Kefir**: around **3 to 4 g protein**, 40 kcal, 120 mg calcium

The headline: **cottage cheese and quark are the protein leaders**, skyr is barely behind and wins on calcium, and Greek yogurt is close on everything. Kefir sits in a different category, lower in protein but valued for its gut-friendly cultures. For pure protein density, the top four are so close that taste and texture should decide.

## How to choose the right one for your goal

Because the protein numbers are so similar, the smarter question is what each one does best.

**For the most protein per calorie**, reach for [cottage cheese](/foods/cottage-cheese/) or quark. Both give you around 12 g of protein per 100 g for very few calories, and both are rich in casein, the slow-digesting protein that makes them a great pre-sleep snack for overnight muscle recovery.

**For calcium alongside protein**, [skyr](/blog/skyr-nutrition/) is the standout. At around 150 mg per 100 g it comfortably beats the others, so it is the densest single choice if you want to support bones and muscle at once. See how it stacks up directly in [skyr vs Greek yogurt](/blog/skyr-vs-greek-yogurt/) and [cottage cheese vs skyr](/compare/cottage-cheese-vs-skyr/).

**For a smooth, creamy texture**, quark and Greek yogurt win. Quark is the European favourite that sits between yogurt and cottage cheese, ideal for spreads, dips and baking. If you find cottage cheese curds off-putting, quark gives you almost identical protein with a smoother finish.

**For versatility**, cottage cheese is unbeaten right now. It blends smooth for sauces, bakes into flatbreads, and works in both sweet and savoury dishes, which is behind its viral popularity. Compare it head to head in [cottage cheese vs Greek yogurt](/compare/cottage-cheese-vs-greek-yogurt/).

**For gut health over protein**, kefir is the pick. It is lower in protein but delivers a wider range of live cultures than yogurt or skyr, so it is the one to choose when digestion, not protein density, is your priority.

## Which is easiest to digest?

If dairy sometimes leaves you bloated, the type matters. Straining and fermentation both lower lactose, so the strained, cultured options are often gentler than milk. **Skyr, quark and Greek yogurt** are strained, which drains off much of the lactose, and their live cultures help break down what remains, so many people who are mildly lactose-sensitive tolerate them well in normal portions. **Cottage cheese** is lower in lactose than milk too, though it is not strained as heavily. None of these are lactose-free, so anyone with a true dairy allergy should avoid them, and those with lactose intolerance are best starting with a small portion to test their own tolerance. If gut comfort is your main goal, plain versions with live cultures are the ones to reach for.

## Protein content is only half the story

Chasing the single highest-protein tub misses the point. All four of the top options, cottage cheese, quark, skyr and Greek yogurt, clear the bar for a genuinely high-protein food, so the real decision comes down to **calcium, texture, sodium and how you like to eat**. Cottage cheese can be higher in sodium, skyr and quark are lower in calories, and Greek yogurt is the most widely available.

The best high-protein dairy is simply the one you will eat regularly. Rotate a couple of them through your week, keep them plain to avoid added sugar, and pair them with fruit, oats or vegetables for a balanced, filling meal.
