## Protein in cottage cheese

Protein is the headline. Cottage cheese delivers around **12 g per 100 g**, and a generous serving can provide **14 to 18 g**. It also contains **casein**, a slow-digesting protein that releases amino acids gradually, which is why many people eat it before bed.

## Calcium and other nutrients

Cottage cheese provides around **83 mg of calcium per 100 g**, a useful contribution to bone health, plus **vitamin B12**, phosphorus and selenium. Its calcium is a little lower than Greek yogurt, but it still adds up across a serving. See our [high-calcium foods list](/food-lists/high-calcium-foods/) for the richest sources.

## Is cottage cheese good for you?

For most people, cottage cheese is an excellent choice:

- **Very high in protein** for muscle and fullness
- **Low in calories and fat**
- **Good source of calcium and B12**
- **Endlessly versatile** in sweet and savoury dishes

The one thing to watch is **sodium**. Regular cottage cheese carries roughly 300 to 400 mg per 100 g, and a half-cup can reach 350 to 450 mg, since salt is added for flavour and to preserve it. That is fine for most people, but worth managing if you have high blood pressure or are cutting salt. Two simple fixes: choose a low-sodium or no-salt-added version, or rinse regular cottage cheese under cold water for a minute or two, which cuts the sodium by 60 to 80 percent while keeping most of the protein and calcium.

## Why is cottage cheese so popular?

Cottage cheese has gone viral because it ticks every box of modern eating: **high protein, low cost, and incredible versatility**. People blend it smooth for creamy pasta sauces and dips, mix it into pancakes and flatbreads, and even turn it into high-protein ice cream. It delivers a protein hit in both sweet and savoury dishes, which fits perfectly with the focus on protein and affordable, healthy food.
